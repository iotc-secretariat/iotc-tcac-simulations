# Catch Data Disclaimer

The catch data used in the TCAC application are derived from the best scientific estimates compiled by the Secretariat of the IOTC. While these datasets represent the most comprehensive information currently available for the Indian Ocean tuna and tuna-like fisheries, they are subject to several sources of uncertainty that vary across time, fleets, and fisheries. In general, data quality and completeness improve markedly in more recent years, reflecting strengthened reporting requirements and enhanced data collection systems. The different sources of uncertainty in the IOTC catch data are reviewed in [IOTC-2025-TCAC15-INF01](https://iotc.org/documents/TCAC/15/INF01) and are summarised below.

## Data Sources

Catch data compiled by the Secretariat originate from multiple sources, including official submissions by CPCs, historical datasets inherited from the Indo-Pacific Tuna Development and Management Programme, and complementary information from the FAO global capture production database. For earlier decades, particularly prior to the establishment of the IOTC and the adoption of mandatory reporting requirements, data gaps are substantial and catches for some fleets and countries were partially or fully estimated using ancillary information.

Over time, the progressive adoption of IOTC data-related resolutions, standardised reporting templates, and verification procedures has resulted in a substantial reduction in unreported and aggregated data, especially for industrial fisheries. Nevertheless, gaps remain for some fleets, non-CPCs, and specific fisheries, and these are addressed through estimation procedures endorsed by the Scientific Committee.

## Raising Process

The raising of catch data to annual totals is subject to uncertainty, particularly for coastal and small-scale fisheries. These fisheries often operate in multi-gear, multi-species contexts, with dispersed landing sites and limited sampling coverage, which can affect the representativeness of samples and the robustness of extrapolation methods. In some cases, catches are not fully raised, or year-to-year variability reflects changes in sampling effort rather than true changes in fishing activity.

Additional uncertainty arises from species identification issues, notably for small tunas and mixed catches. While corrective procedures have been implemented for some industrial fisheries, such corrections are not uniformly available across all fleets or historical periods. Discard data constitute a further source of uncertainty, as discards remain poorly quantified for many fisheries and time periods, despite increasing reporting obligations and recent improvements linked to observer programmes and retention measures.

## Spatial Allocation

Geo-referenced catch data are subject to additional sources of uncertainty related to incomplete spatial reporting, differences in spatial resolution across fleets and time periods, and the use of proxy information to spatially distribute aggregated catches. For historical data and for fisheries with limited logbook or observer coverage, spatial allocation may rely on assumptions based on fleet behaviour, known fishing patterns, or data from comparable years or fisheries.

As a result, spatially resolved catch estimates should be interpreted with caution, particularly for earlier periods and for fisheries characterised by limited monitoring. The reliability of spatial allocation generally improves in more recent years, reflecting increased availability of geo-referenced data and enhanced compliance with IOTC reporting standards.
